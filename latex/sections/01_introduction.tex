Synthetic still images from modern generative models are increasingly difficult to separate from photographs by casual inspection, yet many content-moderation pipelines must run under tight parameter and compute budgets.
The practical question is therefore not only which architecture maximizes in-distribution (ID) accuracy, but which \emph{compact} detector for AI-generated content (AIGC) offers a usable accuracy--efficiency trade-off once images leave the training generators and pass through ordinary recompression.

Prior work has explored CNN detectors~\cite{wang2020cnn,tan2019efficientnet}, frequency-domain cues~\cite{frank2020freq}, pretrained universal detectors~\cite{ojha2023ufd,liu2024fatformer}, reconstruction-based pipelines~\cite{wang2023dire}, large multi-generator benchmarks~\cite{zhu2023genimage}, and compact/efficient detectors such as LAID and LOTA~\cite{chivaran2025laid,wang2025lota}.
Reported gains are often entangled with mismatched training protocols, ImageNet or CLIP initialization, or heterogeneous evaluation sets, which complicates operating-point selection.
Frequency features are sometimes presented as broadly helpful for diffusion artifacts; likewise, strong ID scores can conceal fragile transfer to particular generators or content domains~\cite{ojha2023ufd}.

We conduct a controlled, from-scratch comparison of compact detectors under one shared recipe.
The Panel~A comparison includes:
\begin{enumerate}
\item compact CNN baselines (EfficientNet-B0, MobileNetV3-Small, ShuffleNetV2-x0.5)~\cite{tan2019efficientnet,howard2019mobilenetv3,ma2018shufflenet};
\item a gated frequency-aware CNN (LiteFreqNet~v2) and related ablations;
\item two study-specific pure-PyTorch SSM classifiers (\textbf{LiteSSM-A} and \textbf{LiteSSM-B}), with and without an attached frequency branch.
\end{enumerate}

Evaluation spans ID test performance, content-domain splits (CelebA-HQ vs.\ bedroom), UniversalFakeDetect (UFD) per-generator and Macro averages~\cite{ojha2023ufd}, JPEG quality-70 recompression, and measured efficiency (parameters, FLOPs, batch-1 latency, batch-32 throughput).
We report pretrained UnivFD and NPR separately for context~\cite{ojha2023ufd,tan2024npr}, use \textbf{UFD Macro AUC} as the primary cross-generator summary, and report the unmatched FLUX probe in Appendix~\ref{app:flux}~\cite{flux2024}.

The study combines a shared low-data recipe with generator-, domain-, JPEG-, and efficiency-specific evidence. It also examines failure modes through score distributions, decision agreement, and fixed-weight ensembles.
Within Panel~A, \textbf{LiteSSM-A} attains the highest UFD Macro while retaining moderate batch-32 throughput. Its transfer remains generator dependent: DALL·E stays difficult, bedroom images remain less separable than CelebA-HQ, and the frequency branch does not provide a consistent gain.
